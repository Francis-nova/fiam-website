The call that never should have happened: why two brothers built Fiam

Meet Godwin and Francis Awuya, the brothers from Lagos, Nigeria behind Fiam. Why they built a wallet that protects your money, and what's coming next.

The call that never should have happened

Call her Mama Titi. She is 68, a retired schoolteacher in Lagos, and she has kept her pension in the same bank account for twenty years.

One Tuesday morning her phone rings. A calm voice says he is calling from her bank. Her account has been "flagged", he explains, and unless she confirms a few details it will be frozen by noon.

He sounds kind. He knows her name. He even knows the last four digits of her card.

By the time her son gets home that evening, ₦480,000 is gone.

The bank's customer care line rings out. When someone finally picks up, three days later, they tell her the transfer was "authorised by the account holder". There is nothing they can do.

Mama Titi isn't one person. She is a composite of the stories Godwin and Francis Awuya kept hearing from neighbours, church members and friends' parents across Lagos. And while those stories piled up, the brothers were living their own version: bank transfers that failed, hung for hours, or vanished and reappeared days later with no explanation.

That decision became Fiam. But to understand why it looks the way it does, you need to know who built it, and where.

Meet the founders of Fiam

Fiam is built by two brothers who split the company cleanly down the middle. One owns everything you see and feel. The other owns everything that makes it work.

Godwin Awuya, CEO and Chief Product Officer. Godwin is a senior product designer with seven years' experience across fintech, healthtech and e-commerce. At Fiam he leads product, UX and UI design, branding and frontend development. If you've noticed how quickly the app gets out of your way, that's his work.

Francis Awuya, CTO and COO. Francis is the engineer. He built Fiam's infrastructure and runs the company's operations day to day. Before Fiam he worked at MTN, where he saw first-hand what it takes to run technology that millions of Nigerians rely on every single day.

Both brothers also worked at Nuclesis (now Rigo), which gave them a shared language for building products long before they built their own. Which raises the obvious question: why didn't they start sooner?

From Lagos to Awuya Digital

The Awuya brothers grew up in Lagos, Nigeria. It's the kind of city where everyone runs some kind of business, everyone sends and receives money every day, and everyone has a story about a transfer that went wrong.

In 2022 they started Awuya Digital. For its first few years it was a services company: they designed and built products for clients, sharpening their craft on other people's ideas.

In 2026 they made a deliberate turn. Instead of building for clients, they would build their own products, to solve problems they had lived through.

  • August 2026: Luma, an AI-powered SMS marketing tool for businesses, became the first product out of Awuya Digital.
  • October 2026: Fiam followed: a wallet and payments app built around fast, reliable transfers and protecting users' money.

Why two brothers, and no outsiders at the core

Fiam is a family company by design, not by accident. Over the years, both brothers had been disappointed, and at times cheated, by outside partners. Deals that weren't what they seemed. People who weren't straightforward.

So they made a rule: no more outsiders at the core. They happily work with partners, contractors and advisers, but responsibility for the company's growth sits with them. Nobody else gets to hold Fiam's future.

The second reason is simpler.

Ask about disagreements and both brothers shrug. They think alike and chase the same goals, so decisions move fast.

The hard part: building without a cheque

Here's what most people don't know about Fiam: there is no venture money behind it. It is fully bootstrapped, built on the brothers' own savings, skills and long nights.

That constraint shapes everything. Every feature has to earn its place, and every naira spent has to move the product forward. It's also why Fiam launched lean: Android first, with the core features people use every day.

The plan is deliberate. Get Fiam into people's hands, prove that users stay because it works, and only then raise.

The early numbers are already talking. By Fiam's own figures, transfers land in about one second on average, with a 99% success rate.

The one line they won't cross

There is one thing the Awuya brothers will not trade away: user satisfaction. In practice, that means two promises.

  • You control your money. Not the app, not a hidden rule, not a support queue that never answers.
  • We protect your money. Every transfer needs your PIN and shows you the recipient's name before you confirm. See how Fiam keeps your money safe.

Even the logo carries the promise: a letter F tilted into a bird in flight. Money that moves fast, and stays out of the wrong hands.

What's next for Fiam

Fiam is live on Android today, and the iPhone app is coming soon. Anyone can join the iPhone waitlist for early access.

The bigger move is stablecoins. Fiam is working towards letting you send, receive and convert stablecoins like USDT to naira inside the same wallet you already use for transfers and bills. See what's coming to crypto on Fiam.

Frequently asked questions

Who founded Fiam?

Fiam was founded by brothers Godwin Awuya (CEO and Chief Product Officer) and Francis Awuya (CTO and COO). It is a product of Awuya Digital Limited in Lagos.

What is Awuya Digital?

Awuya Digital is the company behind Fiam, started by the Awuya brothers in 2022. It began as a product design and development services company before launching its own products, Luma in August 2026 and Fiam in October 2026.

Is Fiam funded by investors?

Not yet. Fiam is fully bootstrapped by its founders. They plan to raise once the product has proven traction with users.

Is Fiam available on iPhone?

Fiam is on Android now and the iPhone app is coming soon. You can join the waitlist on the Fiam website for early access.

Will Fiam support crypto?

Fiam is working on stablecoin support so you can send, receive and convert stablecoins such as USDT to naira inside the same wallet.
